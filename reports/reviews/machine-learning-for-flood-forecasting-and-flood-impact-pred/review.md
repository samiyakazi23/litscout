# Machine learning for flood forecasting and flood impact prediction in the Brahmaputra basin

*25 papers reviewed; 58% of cited sentences verified against their sources.*

## ML flood forecasting models

Several reviews highlight the evolution of machine learning (ML) approaches for flood forecasting. Early literature reviews identified hybridization, data decomposition, algorithm ensembles, and model optimization as effective strategies for improving ML flood prediction [1]. Critical reviews of specific algorithms such as ANFIS, ANN, and SVM detail their respective advantages and disadvantages for runoff and flood modeling [2]. More recent analyses compare traditional ML with deep learning (DL) methods, concluding that DL models generally achieve higher accuracy than conventional ML approaches [3]. System‑scale proposals advocate the development of ML systems that leverage transfer or multitask learning to provide timely riverine flood predictions across continents [9][15]. Operational frameworks integrating LSTM, linear, thresholding, and novel manifold models have been deployed in India and Bangladesh, delivering millions of alerts during the 2021 monsoon season [20].

## Brahmaputra basin hydrology

Research specific to the Brahmaputra basin includes a machine‑learning‑based error correction of the GPM IMERG precipitation product, where Random Forest and neural networks reduced systematic and random errors relative to gauge observations [23]. Weekly discharge forecasting for the Brahmaputra‑Jamuna river employed four ML regressors and an ensemble model, achieving high skill (R² = 0.94, NSE = 0.94) using ERA5 reanalysis rainfall and antecedent discharge as inputs [12]. An operational flood‑alert system covering India and Bangladesh incorporated LSTM and other ML components, demonstrating large‑scale applicability in the Brahmaputra region during the 2021 monsoon [20].

## Remote sensing and data assimilation

Advanced frameworks integrate multi‑satellite observations with physics‑informed neural solvers; FloodCast combines satellite SAR and optical data with a geometry‑adaptive neural operator to achieve high‑precision large‑scale flood modeling (average MAPE = 14.93 %) in Pakistan [25]. Deep learning models that fuse satellite imagery and IoT sensor data using CNNs for spatial patterns and LSTMs for temporal dynamics have demonstrated high accuracy in flood forecasting across various flood‑prone regions [13].

## Research gaps

- No studies in the card set evaluate ML‑based flood impact or risk assessment specifically for the Brahmaputra basin, leaving a gap in understanding how impact prediction methods (e.g., LSTM‑based severity models) perform in this region [14] provides a related example but not Brahmaputra‑specific.
- Remote sensing data assimilation for flood forecasting in the Brahmaputra basin is not covered; while FloodCast and satellite‑IoT fusion frameworks show promise [25][13], their application to Brahmaputra hydrology remains unstudied.
- The integration of large‑scale, transfer‑learning or multitask ML systems for riverine flood prediction across the Brahmaputra basin is absent; proposals exist for continent‑wide systems [9][15] but lack basin‑specific implementation or evaluation.
